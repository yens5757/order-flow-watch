## Scope and visitor mode

The calculator page (`/calculator/`, bare or opened from a link that carries inputs: `?n=24&s=2000&mix=50&o=10&c=5000&m=0&b=<slug>`). Home no longer embeds a compact estimator (it links here with its yearly one-liner). Mode: operate. The visitor came to put in their own habits and get one figure back; the world lends type, palette, density and the price scale, never the controls.

## Audience, job, proof, constraints

- Audience: US retail investors, often arriving from a broker page's "Change" button or the nav, on a phone or a desktop; first-timers who know how they trade, not how markets work.
- Job: "What would a year of my trading cost at my broker, and at the others?" One generated figure for their broker, its rank among the brokers whose every needed cost is confirmed, the gap to the cheapest; then every broker.
- Proof on the page: a row per part of the cost with its rate, the neutral price scale, every broker as stacked bars with an HTML key, a sortable table with CSV, the Rule 605 month and the fees check date, How we measure.
- Constraints: `annualCost` (lib/cost.ts) is the only arithmetic; rank only brokers with a complete figure; "At least $X" for an incomplete one; options, idle cash and margin count only after the visitor answers Yes (default No, $0); the yearly cards' priced line reads the idle-cash figure for $1,000 from this same arithmetic (lib/cash.ts), so a broker page's "$38.80 a year less than at Vanguard" is the "Idle cash $38.80" a visitor sees here after Yes and $1,000; a broker that doesn't offer interest on cash (fees.csv NA) reads "Not offered" with "doesn't offer interest on cash, so its idle cash isn't counted" (the options note's form, and the yearly cards' "Cash interest isn't offered"; it said "pays no interest on cash" until the verifier's pass, Nov 2026); a figure below zero reads "$0.22 ahead" ("below the fair price" under Trade prices) in the answer, the table and the bars, never "−$0.22" and no "Below zero" note; invalid input is named inline with `aria-invalid`, the value still in use is stated, absurd values are capped; links carry their inputs and never overwrite saved ones; "Loading your numbers…" guards a query URL; no shared-file edits.

## Composition and memorable moment

Desktop: the broker page's board of two independent stacks (648:448, the right column never under 400px, so from 1024px each number box stays at the end of its row and the two stacks end within about 70px of each other). Left, the answer card: Your broker select; "About $9 a year" with the figure at Display size; the scope line generated from the inputs in use; one row per part; the rank and "See all 25"; the gap to the cheapest; the price scale. Right, "How you trade": three rows for the trades (trades a year, typical size, what you trade), then three Yes/No questions (options, cash at the broker, margin) that each reveal one amount; "Copy link" with its status line in the card's footer. Below, full width: every broker for your trades (key, bars, a note on "at least" lines), the table in dollars, how it adds up.

Phone and tablet: one reading-width stack, the answer first. The inputs card carries a one-line echo of the answer that stays pinned at the top of the screen while the inputs are on it, so a change is seen where it is made.

Placement (reconsidered Nov 2026, kept): one order at every width, the DOM's, never rearranged by CSS: the answer, How you trade, the bars, the table, How it adds up. The answer is the page's job; the inputs shape it, so they sit beside it on a desktop and straight after it on a phone (with the echo). The bars come straight under the board because they spell out its rank and scale; the table follows them whole, with no neighbourhood and no disclosure, because the bars' note and "See all 25" send the visitor there for every broker, the unranked "at least" ones included, and it sorts, searches and downloads as one list. How it adds up is last: the method after the numbers. "Your broker" stays at the head of the answer it changes, and "Copy link" at the foot of the inputs it shares (in the page header, as on the explorer, it would sit above the answer on a phone).

Memorable moment: as the visitor types, picks a mix or answers a question, their broker's dot glides along the scale among the brokers and the figure restates itself in words ("About $9 a year", "At least $9.80 a year", "About $3 ahead a year").

Polish (Nov 2026, the final pass after the three fixes): the scale writes its typical figure like its ends ("Typical $44.00" beside "Public $56.11"; no "about $0.00" at 0 trades), and a scale whose cheapest and most expensive show the same figure (no stock trades) is left out rather than naming a "cheapest" and a "most expensive" at $0. A broker that pays no interest on cash (fees.csv NA) is "cash yield (not offered)" in lib/cost.ts, so the table's Idle cash cell says "Not offered" like the answer card, never "Not confirmed".

The bars in the scale's grammar (third critique, Nov 2026: 20 bars in one saturated orange were the loudest block on the site while the visitor's broker was marked only by a row tint). Every other broker's trade prices take the neutral ramp's second step and the visitor's whole bar is ink (the ramp's first step), its name and total in 700 ink on the Here band, so it stands out however its parts split (the answer card above lists them); a part the visitor adds (options fees, idle cash, margin interest; commissions once one costs anything) stacks after trade prices in its series colour, the explorer's for the same fee, and the HTML key shows only when two or more parts draw bars. Not Mark Grey, though it clears 3:1 on the card (3.6:1 light, 5.3:1 dark): beside the idle-cash pink it falls under the colour-blind floor (OKLab ΔE 5.0 protan, light) and beside the commissions green under the normal-vision one (13.4); ramp-2 clears both themes (ΔE 11.3 and more against every part; 5.6:1 on the light card, 6.5:1 on the dark one), and the whole stack now passes the normal-vision floor the orange-first one failed (12.9 light, 10.6 dark). ## Direction contract

THESIS: The calculator is one calm answer the visitor shapes: what a year of their own trading costs at their broker, in dollars and words, and where that sits among the brokers. It refuses the category defaults: a wall of equal input boxes with the result below the fold, a pre-filled assumption that writes the headline, and a red/green league table.

OWN-WORLD: The established world in DESIGN.md, by reference: soft white cards (24px, 20px on phones) on the teal-tinted Sage Ground (dark: dim sage with lighter cards), Figtree at the seven rem steps, sage-grey ink, the one action teal only on pressable things and focus, Lift shadows, value rows with hairlines, the neutral price scale as the signature, a real table for every chart, words not colour for good and bad. Series colours appear only inside the chart and its key.

STORY: The visitor sees their broker's yearly cost before any input, changes their trades and answers the three questions, watches the figure and the dot respond, checks the parts and the rank, compares every broker in the bars and the table, and copies a link that opens on exactly these numbers.

FIRST VIEWPORT: Header (wordmark, nav with Calculator current, theme toggle); h1 "What does your broker cost you a year?". Desktop: the answer card left (select, "About $9 a year" at Display size, scope line, part rows, rank) and "How you trade" right (trades, size, what you trade, the three questions), both above the fold. Phone: the h1, then the answer card's select, figure, scope line and rows; the inputs start below and pin the echo when scrolled to. Nothing else sticky.
